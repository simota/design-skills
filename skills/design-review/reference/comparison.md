# Reference Comparison

Purpose: How to hold a design against work that already succeeds, and what that comparison is allowed to prove.
Read when: an impression has restated as a principle and the verdict needs a third leg.
Source: none — nothing outside this page can move what it states.
Verified: 2026-08-22 — no automated check.

The third phase, and the easiest to do dishonestly. A comparison locates a gap
precisely; it also imports a whole product's constraints if nobody says it must
not.

## What a comparison proves

| It proves | It does not prove |
|---|---|
| The thing is achievable — somebody shipped it | That it belongs in this product |
| A specific gap exists, at a specific place | That closing the gap is worth its cost |
| The impression was not idiosyncratic | That the reference is good, or current |

**A reference is evidence about possibility, never about fit.** Fit is the
brief's question, and the brief is owned upstream.

## Choosing references

- **Same job, not same industry.** A dense data tool learns more from another
  dense tool than from a competitor's marketing site
- **Two or three, not one.** A single reference becomes a target. Several make
  the shared property visible, which is what actually transfers
- **At least one the product already resembles.** A comparison only against work
  the product could never be is a critique of the budget
- **Name why each was chosen.** An unexplained reference is taste with a
  screenshot attached

## Opening them

**Recalling what a well-known product does is not a comparison.** Memory of an
interface is reconstructed from its reputation, and it drifts toward the ideal.
Open it, capture it under conditions comparable to the ones under review, and
put the two views side by side.

Where it cannot be opened — it is behind a login, it no longer ships, the
capture is dated — say so, and the phase is absent rather than weak.

## Writing it

> **Reference** — two shipped tools of the same density, both captured at the
> same width. **Shared property** — both hold the primary action at the highest
> contrast on the screen, and let everything else recede. **Gap** — here the
> action sits at the same weight as four neighbouring controls.

The shared property is the point. A list of differences is a list of other
people's decisions; the property they have in common is the thing being claimed.

## Traps

- **Comparing to a redesign concept** rather than a shipped product. Concepts
  carry none of the states, the content lengths, or the edge cases
- **Comparing across densities.** A marketing page and an editor disagree about
  everything, and none of it is a defect
- **Letting the reference set the direction.** If the comparison keeps producing
  "be more like X", the real question is what this product is, and that is a
  brief to settle, not a review to file
- **Citing a house style as a threshold.** A platform convention is citable; one
  company's aesthetic is not, however admired
