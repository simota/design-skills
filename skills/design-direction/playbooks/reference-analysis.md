# Reference Analysis

Turn supplied references into principles you can apply, never into an identity you copy.

## Procedure

1. **Collect** — screenshots, links, or product names the user names. Ask for 3–6; fewer gives no signal, more dilutes.
2. **Describe, don't judge** — for each reference record what is observably true.
3. **Extract principles** — the repeated strategy behind the surface.
4. **Decide inheritance** — adopt, invert, or ignore each principle, with a reason.

## Per-reference record

| Dimension | What to record |
|-----------|----------------|
| Density | Information per screen; generous, balanced, or packed |
| Type strategy | One family or paired; weight range used; display/body contrast |
| Colour strategy | Monochrome+accent, duotone, full palette; where saturation appears |
| Surface | Borders vs shadows vs flat fills; corner language |
| Composition | Grid regularity, asymmetry, whitespace distribution |
| Motion | Present/absent; functional or expressive |
| Restraint | What it visibly refuses to do |

## Extraction to principle

Bad: "uses purple." Useless — a value, not a strategy.
Good: "restricts saturation to a single accent used only for the primary action; all state colour is carried by weight and border, not hue."

A principle is portable across palettes. If your extraction stops working when you change the hex values, you extracted a value, not a principle.

## Inheritance decision

| Decision | Meaning | Record as |
|----------|---------|-----------|
| Adopt | The principle serves our adjectives | "Adopt: single-accent restraint — supports 'focused'" |
| Invert | The opposite serves us better | "Invert: they are spacious; we are dense — our users scan 200 rows" |
| Ignore | Irrelevant to our constraints | "Ignore: their motion budget assumes native; we ship web" |

## Boundaries

- Do not reproduce a recognisable logo, wordmark, proprietary illustration style, or a competitor's identity as a whole. Extract and abstract.
- If a user asks for "make it look exactly like <brand>", say plainly that you will match the underlying strategy and craft level rather than the identity, then do that.
- Note licensing when a reference's distinctiveness comes from a commercial typeface.

## Conflicting references

When references pull apart (e.g. an austere tool and an expressive consumer app), do not average them — averaging is how directions become generic. Instead:

1. Name the conflict explicitly in the brief.
2. Assign each reference a scope ("austere in the app shell, expressive in marketing").
3. Or make it a fork: two of the three considered directions, and let the user pick.
